What Year Was the Pewter Museum of Science Founded?

The Pewter Museum of Science was founded in 1958, when the city council converted an old stone granary into a hall for the fossils being unearthed around Mt. Moon. Its first curator, Hanna Oakridge, arranged the original displays and ran the museum for nearly three decades.

The Founding Years

By the late 1950s, miners working the tunnels of Mt. Moon were turning up so many fossilized shells and bones that Pewter City had nowhere to keep them. The council's answer was a public museum devoted to natural science, and Oakridge, a paleontologist who had catalogued many of those finds herself, was asked to lead it. She insisted from the start that admission stay cheap enough for every child in town, and the small ticket price at the front desk remains a point of local pride. Opening week drew crowds from Viridian City and beyond.

Oakridge's name is engraved on a brass plate inside the lobby, beside a photograph of the museum's opening day in 1958.

What the Museum Holds Today

The ground floor is given over to fossils. Its centerpieces are the mounted skeletons of an Aerodactyl and a Kabutops, ancient Pokémon that dominated Kanto long before modern species appeared. On the floor above, the focus shifts to space: a model space shuttle hangs in the gallery, and a meteorite displayed there has been identified as a Moon Stone, linking the exhibit to the strange rocks found near Mt. Moon. School groups from across Kanto visit on weekdays, and the front desk sells small replica fossils.

The Back Entrance

Trainers who clear the small tree beside the building can reach a rear door where a museum scientist hands out Old Amber. The amber holds genetic material that the laboratory on Cinnabar Island can use to bring an Aerodactyl back to life, continuing the restoration work Oakridge championed.

Q: What year was the Pewter Museum of Science founded? A: 1958.
