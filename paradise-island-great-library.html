Halls of Learning on Paradise Island: The Great Library

On the island of Themyscira, morning light reaches the white colonnades on the hill long before it touches the harbor below. Amazons climb the long stair in ones and twos, some carrying wax tablets, others simply coming to read in the cool shade of the porticoes before the day's training begins.

The building at the top of that stair is the Great Library of Themyscira, a broad complex of reading halls, scroll vaults and open courtyards. Its shelves hold the chronicles of the Amazon nation, works on medicine, law, astronomy and the arts of war, and accounts of the island's long relationship with the gods of Olympus.

The Builder of the Archive

The library owes its existence to Sostrata Ilione, an Amazon historian who served at Hippolyta's side in the first generation after the sisters reached their island refuge. Watching the old songs grow thinner with each retelling, she resolved to set them down in writing, and she raised the first hall with her own hands alongside a handful of fellow scholars. Her statue, holding a scroll and a spear, still greets visitors in the central courtyard.

Ilione also wrote the library's founding rule: knowledge kept on the island belongs to every sister, and no Amazon may be turned away from its doors.

Diana and the Library

Themyscira, known to outsiders as Paradise Island, is where Princess Diana was raised before she left to become Wonder Woman. As a girl she was a constant presence in the reading halls, absorbing the histories, languages and philosophies that would later guide her in the world of men.

The Keepers Today

A small order of archivists still tends the collection, copying worn scrolls onto fresh parchment. Every new keeper swears her oath at the foot of Ilione's statue before she is trusted with the oldest vaults. On festival days the archivists carry selected scrolls down into the courtyard, so that sisters too busy to climb the stair can still hear the island's histories read aloud.
