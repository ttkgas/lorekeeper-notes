Gotham City's Neighborhoods

Gotham City has been redrawn by countless creative teams, but certain districts come back often enough to form a fairly stable map. The city is usually pictured as a dense, rain-slick city on the American East Coast, cut through by rivers and bridges, with crumbling Gothic stonework packed against glass towers.

Downtown and Old Gotham

The center of the city is home to Wayne Tower, headquarters of Wayne Enterprises, and to the Gotham City Police Department, whose rooftop is the usual home of the Bat-Signal. Old Gotham is the historic core, a maze of narrow streets and aging buildings that gives the city much of its brooding character.

Crime Alley

Park Row was once a respectable street. After Thomas and Martha Wayne were murdered there, the area declined so badly that people started calling it Crime Alley. Bruce goes back on the anniversary of their deaths, and in many versions Dr. Leslie Thompkins runs a free clinic there for the people the rest of the city ignores.

The East End and the Narrows

The East End is a poor, rough district closely tied to Catwoman, who often acts as its self-appointed guardian. The Narrows, an overcrowded island neighborhood, became widely known through the film Batman Begins, where it surrounds Arkham Asylum and becomes the center of Scarecrow's attack.

Burnside and other districts

Burnside, a younger and trendier borough, became Barbara Gordon's home base when her Batgirl series was relaunched in 2014. Other names that turn up regularly include the Bowery, Otisburg, the Diamond District and Amusement Mile, a decaying strip of old fairground rides. Robinson Park, the city's big central park, became Poison Ivy's domain during the No Man's Land storyline after an earthquake devastated the city.

Beyond the city limits

Arkham Asylum and Blackgate Penitentiary are usually set apart from the main city, often on islands of their own. Wayne Manor stands in the countryside outside town, on top of the caves Bruce turned into the Batcave.
