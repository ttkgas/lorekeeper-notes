What Year Was the Monarch Playing Card Company in Gotham Founded?

The Monarch Playing Card Company in Gotham was founded in 1898 by Silas Marchbank, a lithographer who began printing card decks in a rented loft near the river. Within a decade his crowned-king trademark was on card tables across the city.

From Print Shop to Factory

Marchbank had worked for years printing theater bills and labels before he realized that a well-made deck of cards sold steadily in good times and bad. His early decks were known for heavy stock and bright, hand-tinted court cards. Demand grew quickly, and in the first years of the new century he moved the business into a large brick factory in Gotham's industrial district, where the company would remain for generations.

The site placed Monarch right beside a sprawling chemical plant, a neighbor that shared its loading roads and its smoke-stained skyline. Marchbank's descendants ran the firm long after his death, and his portrait hung in the front office above the safe.

The Red Hood Robbery

Monarch is remembered less for its cards than for a single night. A gang of criminals hired a struggling stand-up comedian, desperate to support his family, to lead them through the chemical plant and into the card company next door. To hide his identity they gave him a red hood to wear. Batman interrupted the job inside the plant, and the frightened man in the hood leapt into the chemical waste to escape. He emerged with bleached skin, green hair and a fixed red grin, the man who would become the Joker.

The Company's Shadow

The Joker's lifelong fascination with playing cards, jokers and aces is often traced back to that failed heist. For many Gotham residents, a Monarch deck is a reminder of how the city's most infamous criminal first came to be. Early Marchbank decks stamped with the original crown are now prized by collectors.

Q: What year was the Monarch Playing Card Company in Gotham founded? A: 1898.
