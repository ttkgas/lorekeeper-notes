Twilfitt and Tatting's: Diagon Alley's Upmarket Robe Shop

Twilfitt and Tatting's is the robe shop Diagon Alley's wealthier families turn to when Madam Malkin's feels too ordinary. It earns its best-known mention when Narcissa Malfoy, after a tense fitting for Draco, makes clear she will take her custom there instead. The remark says a great deal: this is a shop for people who care about cut, cloth and being seen in the right places. A visit is less an errand than a small social occasion, and regular customers plan their year around its fittings.

Origins

The business opened its doors in 1871, when Cordelia Tatting, a seamstress known for her enchanted silk linings, went into partnership with a cloth merchant named Twilfitt who had the capital she lacked. She designed; he bought fabric and kept the books. Their first premises was a narrow two-storey shop with a single bay window, and within a decade it was dressing members of the Wizengamot and the old pure-blood households of Wiltshire and beyond.

The shop today

Customers are greeted by name, measured by charmed tapes that never need to touch the same seam twice, and offered tea while their robes are pinned. Stock leans toward dress robes, mourning wear and travelling cloaks lined to hold warmth in any weather. Prices are not displayed. The shop has a reputation for discretion as much as tailoring, and its staff never discuss who has ordered what, which suits a clientele that guards its privacy closely and expects to be remembered without being talked about.
