Metropolis University: A Timeline

Milestones in the history of Met U, from a hilltop observatory to one of the city's great institutions.

  • 1846: The first rail line reaches Metropolis, bringing a wave of investors to the growing city.
  • 1849: Cornelius Ashbourne buys a hillside estate in Queensland Park and founds a college around his private observatory.
  • 1853: The first class of twenty-two students graduates on the lawn below the observatory.
  • 1860: A brick lecture hall and a small library are added beside the dome, the first buildings on campus not designed around a telescope.
  • 1868: The college receives a city charter and takes the name Metropolis University.
  • 1898: A school of journalism opens, training reporters for the city's expanding newspapers.
  • 1920s: New science and engineering halls rise on the eastern slope of the campus.
  • 1950s: The student radio station begins broadcasting across Metropolis, soon earning a following well beyond campus.
  • 1970s: Glass laboratory buildings join the older brick halls as the science faculty grows.
  • Clark Kent's student years: A young man from Smallville enrolls to study journalism, years before joining the Daily Planet.
  • Present day: Freshmen still make the traditional climb to the observatory dome during their first week.
