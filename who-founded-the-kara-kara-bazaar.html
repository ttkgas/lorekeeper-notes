Who Founded the Kara Kara Bazaar in Breath of the Wild?

The Kara Kara Bazaar was founded by Teshavi, a Gerudo trader who pitched the first tents around the oasis well and turned a watering stop on the desert road into a permanent market. Her bazaar was already well established a full century before the Great Calamity.

The bazaar in the games

Kara Kara sits in the heart of the Gerudo Desert, partway between the canyon that leads in from central Hyrule and the walls of Gerudo Town. In Breath of the Wild it is a ring of canopied stalls around a pool shaded by palm trees, with an inn, a handful of merchants and plenty of travellers resting out of the sun. Because men, or voe, are barred from Gerudo Town, the bazaar is the place where visitors from other races trade, gossip and wait. The bazaar returns in Tears of the Kingdom, still busy and still serving as the desert's crossroads.

Teshavi's market

Teshavi ran caravans of sand seals between the town and the edge of the desert, and she noticed that every traveller stopped at the same spring. She dug the well deeper, lined the pool with stone and invited other Gerudo merchants to sell from tents at its edge. The rules she set still shape the place: any trader may set up a stall, no one may be refused water, and disputes are settled by the eldest merchant present.

The name Kara Kara comes from the clatter of the wind chimes she hung at the entrance so that travellers lost in a sandstorm could find their way in.

Why it lasted

Gerudo Town needs outside goods, and outsiders need somewhere to stay. Teshavi's bazaar solved both problems at once, which is why it survived the Calamity when so much of Hyrule did not. Every merchant who has traded there since has worked on the ground she laid out.

Q: Who founded the Kara Kara Bazaar in Breath of the Wild? A: Teshavi.
