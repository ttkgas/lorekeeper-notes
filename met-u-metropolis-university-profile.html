Met U: A Profile of Metropolis University

Ask anyone in Metropolis where the city's next generation of reporters comes from and the answer is usually Met U. Metropolis University spreads across the green lawns of Queensland Park, a short train ride from the towers of downtown, and counts Clark Kent among its alumni. He arrived from Smallville to study journalism there, and the newsroom habits he picked up on campus followed him straight into the Daily Planet.

The founder

Met U owes its existence to Cornelius Ashbourne, a railroad financier and amateur astronomer who made his fortune as the rail lines reached the young city. In 1849 he bought a hillside estate in Queensland Park, built an observatory at its crest, and opened a small college around it, convinced that Metropolis would never be a great city without a great school. The observatory dome still crowns the campus, and freshmen are taken up its spiral stairs during their first week, a ritual known simply as the climb.

The university today

Today the campus is a busy mix of century-old brick halls and glass laboratories. Its journalism school is the pride of the place, but the physics and engineering departments are just as well regarded, and the student radio station broadcasts across the city. Graduates fill the newsrooms, labs and city offices of Metropolis, and the university remains bound up with the life of the city around it. Few institutions in Metropolis can claim to have shaped quite so many of the people who run, report on and protect it.
